# Finn Mallery

*Founder of Origami, a Y Combinator company whose product powers lead generation and outbound for sales teams. Interviewed the ten fastest-growing YC companies about how they got customers and distilled the playbook they share. Source on customer acquisition.*

---

## Background

Finn Mallery is the founder of Origami, a Y Combinator alum whose product pulls prospect lists and powers targeted outbound for B2B sales teams, including the sales teams of some of the fastest-growing companies from his own accelerator cohort. He practices the acquisition playbook he documents: months of all-day customer calls at the start of the company, hundreds of posts a month held to a single niche, and years of running LinkedIn connection outreach at the platform's weekly ceiling.

His contribution to this wiki comes from field research: he interviewed the ten fastest-growing Y Combinator companies, from the current batch's breakouts to multi-billion-dollar alumni like Corgi and Legora, expecting ten different answers about growth and finding one shared, near-identical playbook.

## Why he is on this wiki

The playbook he distilled is the clearest current answer to the first question every founder of an agentic business faces: where the customers actually come from. Its four steps each earned a principle page here: pick a fight you can win, burn the fog off the market with conversation volume, aim content at one customer profile until the algorithm learns who to deliver you to, and harvest the warm majority who never book on their own.

What earns the material a place on a principles wiki is that it is mechanism rather than motivation. Each step names the structural reason it works: the market-size and incumbent-quality asymmetry of dusty industries, expert intuition as a function of conversation count, interest-graph distribution replacing follower-graph distribution, and engagement signals as consent for outreach.

## Principles contributed to this wiki

- **[Pick an Easy Fight](/principles/pick-an-easy-fight)**: the winners rebuild massive dusty industries with AI instead of building software for software people.
- **[Get Pathologically Close to the Customer](/principles/pathologically-close-to-the-customer)**: the market is a fogged Battleship board, and hundreds of conversations manufacture ten years of intuition in months.
- **[Train the Algorithm, Not an Audience](/principles/train-the-algorithm-not-an-audience)**: platforms push by interest graph now, so hyper-specific content aimed at one customer profile is a free targeted channel.
- **[It's Not Cold If They Liked Your Post](/principles/not-cold-if-they-liked-your-post)**: warm outbound to the 95 percent of the iceberg that content warms but never converts.

## Field Notes

### 2026-08-08 — "I asked 10 of YC's fastest-growing founders how they got customers" (YouTube video)

Mallery's synthesis of interviews with the ten fastest-growing YC companies, run as a single four-step formula. Lifted here: the easy-fight category thesis (Corgi in insurance, Legora in law, Candid Health in medical billing, Arini on the dental front desk), the Battleship frame for customer discovery with Sim and PlanGrid as worked examples, the content-as-algorithm-training doctrine with his own conversion data, and the 95-5 iceberg with Goji Berry's layered content plus LinkedIn warm outbound.

> "You are not building an audience. You are training an algorithm on who you should push content to."

> — Finn Mallery, [I asked 10 of YC's fastest-growing founders how they got customers](https://youtu.be/4OQd_buPjnE), YouTube, 2026-08-08
