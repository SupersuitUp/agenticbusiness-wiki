# Max Hodak

*CEO of Science, whose retinal prosthesis restores vision to patients blinded by the loss of their rods and cones. Co-founded Neuralink and spent five years running it. Source on infrastructure as the basis of speed, action as the cure for stuckness, and judgment as the founder's non-delegable load.*

---

## Background

Max Hodak is the CEO of Science, the brain-computer interface company he founded in 2021 with a team drawn largely from Neuralink, which he co-founded and helped run for five years as president. Science's lead product is a retinal prosthesis: a photovoltaic chip implanted under the retina that, paired with camera glasses and an infrared laser projector, restores vision to patients who have gone blind from the loss of their photoreceptors. The device has been through three clinical trials, carries an approved indication in Europe, published trial results in the New England Journal of Medicine, and put one of its patients on the cover of Time. One patient finished a 300-page novel with the device and mailed the company the book.

He started in neuroscience as a Duke undergraduate doing brain-computer interface research in a primate lab, and has spent roughly two decades on the thesis that engineering access to the brain is one of the great projects of the next several decades.

## Why he is on this wiki

Hodak runs one of the hardest categories of company that exists: implanted medical devices, regulated in multiple jurisdictions, manufacturing included, where a single experimental iteration can cost $40,000. From inside that constraint he arrived at conclusions about speed that apply to every operator here. Deep tech companies rarely die because the technology fails; they die because the organization does. The systems a company runs on (purchasing, hiring, budgeting, performance review) are its operating system, and they set the iteration rate that decides everything downstream.

His company runs on Helix, internal software where nearly everything the company does is a button and every record links to every other, built in-house on the argument that a harness fit exactly to the company is worth more than any purchasable system, and that agents have made building one rational. That makes him one of the clearest living practitioners of the agentic-business thesis in physical-world deep tech.

## Principles contributed to this wiki

- **[Speed Is Determined by Infrastructure](/principles/speed-is-determined-by-infrastructure)**: speed determines success and failure, and the boring systems set the speed. Companies die of organization, not technology.
- **[Action Produces Information](/principles/action-produces-information)**: it is very difficult to actually get stuck. The action space is always larger than it appears, and acting generates the information thinking cannot.
- **[You Cannot Delegate Your Judgment](/principles/you-cannot-delegate-your-judgment)**: years in, the decisive call arrives and there is nobody to ask. Differentiated judgment is the only path to outlier outcomes.

## Field Notes

### 2026-08-08 — "What Really Kills Deep Tech Startups?" (Y Combinator Startup School talk)

A talk about infrastructure that spends almost no time on Science's implants and nearly an hour on purchasing, hiring, reviews, and judgment. Lifted here: the operating-system framing of company support systems, the anatomy of purchasing and hiring at scale, the eigen-reviews mechanism (continuous, graph-weighted peer voting in place of the annual 360 cycle), rate of iteration as the separator of success from failure, the build-your-harness argument for internal software, gathering all company context into one place agents can use, the physics framing of action as the generator of information, and the eerie years-in moment when judgment cannot be outsourced.

> "Speed determines success and failure, and speed is determined by infrastructure."
>
> — Max Hodak, [What Really Kills Deep Tech Startups?](https://youtu.be/Xc4klGbq8v8), YC Startup School, 2026-08-08
