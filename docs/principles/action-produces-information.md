# Action Produces Information

*It is very difficult to actually get stuck: the action space is always larger than it appears. When the company stalls, inject action, because doing things generates information that more thinking never will.*

## What it is

Founders spend enormous energy anticipating problems they will never actually hit, and then freeze when they run into a real limitation. Hodak's counter, from years of running deep tech companies:

> "It's very difficult to actually get stuck. You can get yourself into trouble, and the action space is always larger than it appears."
>
> — Max Hodak, 2026-08-08

When you do hit a real wall, there are always a hundred ideas about how to make the system better. The failure mode is not running out of moves. It is stalling in analysis while the board waits.

## The physics frame

Hodak grounds the principle in the physical quantity called action. A thrown ball follows a ballistic arc; the whole trajectory is determined the moment it leaves the hand, so the flight adds no information. The only way the path changes is if something spends energy acting on it. Exerting action into the universe creates information in a fundamental sense, and a company that is stuck is a company on a ballistic trajectory: fully determined, learning nothing. The way out is to start injecting action, producing entropy, perturbing the system until it starts telling you things again.

> "When you get stuck, you have to start doing things."
>
> — Max Hodak, 2026-08-08

This is why [rate of iteration](/principles/speed-is-determined-by-infrastructure) compounds the way it does: every cycle is an act, and every act returns information that thinking alone would never have produced. A company iterating weekly is running a hundred experiments on reality while the monthly company is still forecasting.

## The counterintuitive unblock

The principle produces moves that feel wrong from inside the stall. Hodak's example: a company stuck in a deep local minimum, where one person was excellent in many ways but the wrong fit for what the company needed at that moment. Removing them, despite their individual strength, unblocked the whole organization and let it enter a new phase. No amount of deliberation would have surfaced that; the information only existed on the far side of the action.

The general shape: when the system is stuck, the highest-value move is often the one that changes the system's configuration rather than the one that optimizes within it. Ship the ugly version. Kill the project that everyone is politely waiting out. Reorganize the team. Run the experiment you have been pricing instead of pricing it again.

## How to apply it

- **Diagnose stalls as information starvation.** If the team has been deliberating for weeks, the missing input is an act, never another analysis.
- **Prefer the move that generates signal.** Between two options, take the one that will teach you the most fastest, even if it is messier.
- **Audit your anticipations.** Most pre-solved problems never arrive. Solve them when reality raises them, with the information reality provides.
- **Act on the misfit.** When the org is stuck around a person, a project, or a structure everyone can name but nobody will touch, that is usually the square where action produces the most information.
