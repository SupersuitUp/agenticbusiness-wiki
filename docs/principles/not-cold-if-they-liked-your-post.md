# It's Not Cold If They Liked Your Post

*Content converts about 5 percent of the people it warms. The other 95 percent saw you, trust you a little, and never booked. Outbound aimed at that iceberg is not cold outreach; it is harvesting.*

## What it is

Content has a structural ceiling: of the people in your customer profile who see it and start to trust you, only a sliver ever book the call or sign up on their own. Mallery calls it the 95-5 iceberg. The 5 percent above the waterline convert themselves. The 95 percent below are people who liked a post, commented, followed you, subscribed, signed up for the free plan, or visited the site, and then did nothing.

The fastest-growing company in Mallery's most recent YC batch comparison, Goji Berry, went from zero to $4 million in annual revenue in under a year on exactly two moves: layered content for the 5 percent, and hyper-targeted warm outbound to the 95 percent.

> "It's not cold if they liked your post, if they commented on your post, if they signed up, if they showed up on your website, if they subscribed to your email newsletter. That's a warm call."
>
> — Finn Mallery, 2026-08-08

## Why the channel works

Every engagement signal is a hand raised halfway. The person already knows who you are, already consumes your thinking, already has the problem your content keeps describing. Outreach to them opens mid-conversation instead of from zero. As the content practice grows, the signals compound into hundreds or thousands of warm leads a week, and cold outbound stops being necessary at all: the loop is post, warm, harvest, post again.

The channel Goji Berry rode is LinkedIn rather than email, for a structural reason: LinkedIn is a social feed, so there is no spam folder between you and the prospect. Messages get seen. A real profile with real content behind it carries credibility that a stranger's email address never will; the prospect sees a person, and usually one they already recognize from the feed.

## How to apply it

- **Track every engagement signal.** Likers, commenters, new followers, site visitors, free-plan signups, newsletter subscribers. This is the warm-lead list; losing it is losing the 95 percent.
- **Work the LinkedIn connection budget.** Roughly 200 new connection requests a week is the sustainable ceiling. Aimed at the customer profile, with about half accepting, that is a hundred warm conversations opened per week, automatable within the platform's limits.
- **Personalize every message.** The weekly budget is capped, so every shot counts. These are high-value prospects who already half-know you; a generic blast burns the warmth that made the channel work.
- **Call the engagers.** Mallery recommends cold calling on the same logic: dialing someone who liked your post three times is a warm call wearing a cold call's clothes.

The prerequisite is a content practice that generates the iceberg in the first place: [Train the Algorithm, Not an Audience](/principles/train-the-algorithm-not-an-audience). Run together, the two form a complete acquisition loop that cost the fastest-growing YC companies nothing but consistency.
