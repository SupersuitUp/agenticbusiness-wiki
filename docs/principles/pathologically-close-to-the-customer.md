# Get Pathologically Close to the Customer

*The market is a fogged Battleship board. Every customer conversation clears one square. Hundreds of conversations manufacture ten years of intuition in a couple of months, and almost nobody has the stomach to run that many.*

## What it is

"Talk to your customers" is the most common advice in startups, and the version most founders practice is too weak to matter. The founders who built multi-billion-dollar companies in industries they had never worked in ran a different intensity of the same idea: conversation volume as a deliberate instrument for burning off the fog of an unfamiliar market.

Mallery's frame is the board game Battleship. Entering an industry, the whole board is fog. You have guesses about where the customer's real problems sit, but pretending to know leads you astray. Each conversation clears one square:

> "Every single customer that you talk to is pretty much removing the fog on one small part of that grid. And it's a massive grid. Most of the shots that you take are going to be misses."
>
> — Finn Mallery, 2026-08-08

The misses are not failures. A customer telling you your solution is irrelevant is a cleared square. The map of where value does not live is most of the map of where it does.

> "The more people that tell you your solution sucks, this isn't a problem, the closer you get to finding out where the real value lies."
>
> — Finn Mallery, 2026-08-08

## Why volume is the whole trick

The founders in this cohort entered industries staffed by people with 30 years of experience. They out-learned them anyway, because a veteran is an expert in their own lived experience: one seat, one company, one workflow. A founder who runs hundreds of conversations across the whole industry becomes an expert in the entire problem space, which squares have been solved, which are still fog, and where the untapped value sits. That map is something no individual insider has.

Most people stop after twenty conversations, because by then they feel like experts. The edge only arrives at hundreds of data points. The founder of Sim spent months on ten calls a day, every day, with a booking link on the landing page and no qualification filter, while the product improved in parallel. After a couple of months the company had 20,000 GitHub stars and a wedge into a market with an entrenched multi-billion-dollar incumbent. That is what manufacturing ten years of intuition in a couple of months looks like in practice.

## When the customer will not get on a call

The move does not change; the venue does. Corgi's and Legora's founders physically sat in their customers' offices and watched them work, because insurance brokers and lawyers were not booking Zoom calls with strangers. PlanGrid's founder brought donuts to construction sites and traded lunch for a hearing with construction workers, and the company later sold for $875 million. Get on the flight, go to the conference, sit in the office, bring the food. The friction is the moat: billion-dollar gaps persist in exactly the industries where getting close to the customer is inconvenient.

## How to apply it

- **Instrument for volume.** A public booking link, no qualification, and a standing rule: any interested person gets a call. Ten a day is the reference intensity, sustained for months.
- **Log the misses.** Track what each conversation eliminated, so cleared squares stay cleared.
- **Hunt value, not validation.** The question is where the biggest unsolved problems sit across the whole board for this customer profile. You cannot build a huge company without finding a square nobody has hit.
- **Keep going past the point of feeling expert.** Feeling like an expert at conversation twenty is the trap. The differentiated map starts at a few hundred.

This is the founder-scale version of [Go and See, Two Eyes Two Ears](/principles/go-and-see): direct observation over interpreted data, applied to market discovery rather than operations. What the cleared board reveals is the transaction to build backwards from, which is [The Magic Moment](/principles/the-magic-moment).
