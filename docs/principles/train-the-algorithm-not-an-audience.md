# Train the Algorithm, Not an Audience

*Social platforms push content by interest graph now, so follower counts barely matter. Hyper-specific content aimed at one customer profile teaches the algorithm exactly who to deliver you to, for free.*

## What it is

The most universal channel across the fastest-growing YC companies is content, and every one of them uses it the same non-obvious way. The goal is never virality and never an audience. The goal is teaching the platform's recommendation system which narrow group of people to deliver your posts to.

> "You are not building an audience. You are training an algorithm on who you should push content to."
>
> — Finn Mallery, 2026-08-08

The mechanism: X, LinkedIn, and the rest rebuilt themselves around TikTok-style For You feeds. Distribution used to flow from your follower graph; now it flows from the interest graph. Good content gets pushed to the people it matches whether you have 80 followers or 80,000. The platforms read your profile and your posting history to decide what you are an expert in, then serve your posts to people interested in that thing. Stay hyper-specific and the platform becomes a free, targeted delivery channel straight to your buyer. Drift off-topic, posting day-in-my-life material from an account that taught the algorithm something else, and your reach gets throttled.

## Impressions are not the metric

Mallery's own numbers make the case. A post that pulled nearly 2 million impressions, on-topic but broad, generated zero revenue. A post with 100,000 impressions, twenty times fewer, added $6,000 in monthly recurring revenue in a day, because it spoke to the exact customer profile about the exact problem.

Founders of billion-dollar companies post daily and collect 20 or 30 likes, and keep doing it, because the arithmetic works without virality. Two targeted posts a day across the platforms your customer uses might be 60,000 impressions a month, with most of the viewers inside your customer profile. If that books five or ten calls a week and closes a handful of serious contracts a month, content alone carries seven figures of pipeline a year at zero spend. Twenty likes that include one enterprise prospect beat a million impressions of memes.

## The compounding loop

The practice is study, remix, repeat. Collect the posts going viral inside your niche, study their structure, and remix them in your own voice loaded with what your customer conversations taught you ([Get Pathologically Close to the Customer](/principles/pathologically-close-to-the-customer) supplies the raw material; the posts are the fog-cleared board made public). A few hours at the start of the week can plan weeks of posting. Consistency compounds twice: you get slightly better at the craft every week, and the platform weights your account more heavily as it learns your lane, so the same quality of post reaches further every month.

The discipline that makes it work is refusing to stray. Mallery posts hundreds of times a month and never leaves growth tactics for B2B founders. That narrowness is what keeps the algorithm's model of the account sharp.

## How to apply it

- **Pick the one customer profile and the one topic, and never post outside it.** The account is a targeting signal before it is a publication.
- **Judge posts by pipeline, not impressions.** A post that books two calls beat the post that got a million views.
- **Post daily on every platform your customer actually uses.** Volume trains the algorithm; silence lets its model of you decay.
- **Remix proven structures with proprietary insight.** Viral form, customer-conversation substance.

Content converts only the small fraction of warmed viewers who act on their own. The completing move for everyone else is [It's Not Cold If They Liked Your Post](/principles/not-cold-if-they-liked-your-post).
