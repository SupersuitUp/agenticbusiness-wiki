# You Cannot Delegate Your Judgment

*Years into the company, with everything on the line, a decision arrives that only you can make, and there is nobody to ask. Averaging over advice drags you toward the class average, and the successful companies are the exceptions.*

## What it is

A founder can delegate almost everything: execution, functions, whole divisions. The one load that never transfers is judgment.

> "As the CEO, you must always make decisions that make sense to you, no matter how much momentum or inertia alternatives seem to have."
>
> — Max Hodak, 2026-08-08

Hodak's school analogy: cheat off the student next to you and your grade gets dragged toward the class average. In startups the same mechanism operates through advice, consensus, and momentum, and it is fatal at the tail, because the average outcome for a startup is failure. The successful companies are the exceptions.

> "In order to succeed, your judgment has to be differentiatedly good. Now, the reality might be that you don't know if your judgment is good yet. One way or another you will have to find out, and that means making decisions that make sense to you even when you are totally alone in that realization. That is the only way to get to the really big outcomes."
>
> — Max Hodak, 2026-08-08

## The eerie moment

Early on, most problems are easily advised: fundraising mechanics, hiring processes, pricing. That abundance of available answers disguises what is coming.

> "You'll get to a key point years in, and you'll look for advice, and there is nobody to ask. And at that point you must have a really good sense of the limits and boundaries of your judgment. That is a very eerie feeling, and you have to be able to commit to it regardless."
>
> — Max Hodak, 2026-08-08

Hundreds of millions of dollars on the line, a genuinely novel fact pattern, and every person qualified to advise you either works for you, is conflicted, or has never seen this situation either. The frontier is definitionally the place where the answer does not exist yet. That is the job.

## Training the filters

Judgment being non-delegable does not mean it is untrainable. Hodak spent five years working directly under a CEO with empirically excellent judgment before starting Science, and describes the education precisely: getting into real trouble with real stakes, forming his own read on option A versus option B, hearing the call, then watching reality grade it. Startup judgment is an oral tradition, passed down by working alongside people who have it, with stakes attached and feedback arriving later. This is also his warning against pattern-matching on generic advice: there are no five bullet points that make a company succeed, only a long series of judgment calls, and the quality of the filters making them.

The same talk supplies the other half of the balance: professionals sweat logistics, and [infrastructure sets the company's speed](/principles/speed-is-determined-by-infrastructure). Systems can carry the operating load. What they cannot do is make the call that has no precedent. Build the systems so that the judgment calls are the only load you personally carry.

## How to apply it

- **Take advice as input, never as a vote.** Collect every perspective available, then make the decision that makes sense to you. If you cannot explain the decision in your own reasoning, you have not made it yet.
- **Apprentice under proven judgment before you need your own.** Working next to someone whose calls reality keeps confirming is the fastest way to fit your filters.
- **Map the boundaries of your judgment early.** Know the domains where your read is differentiated and the ones where it is merely average, before the eerie moment arrives.
- **Expect the lonely call and pre-commit to making it.** The decision nobody can help with is not a failure state. It is the moment the company is paying you for.

The principle bounds [Relinquish Control for Leverage](/principles/relinquish-control-for-leverage): hand off every function, every mechanism, every seat, and keep the one thing that was never transferable.
